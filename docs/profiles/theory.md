# Theory and interpretation

Use entities for authors and institutions, sources for works, concepts for independently useful ideas, and syntheses for comparisons or questions spanning works.

Attribute an idea before explaining it. Distinguish the author's vocabulary, the author's empirical claims, the curator's interpretation, and a personal hypothesis. A concept label is not proof of the framework.

Preserve disagreements and changes in an author's position. Do not invent a blended consensus. Distinguish explicit influence from a proposed similarity.

Exact quotes require an original source and locator. If exact wording is not established, paraphrase and label it as a paraphrase. Automatic transcripts can contain errors.

Split notes for independent retrieval and clear scope. Do not enforce a quota of concepts, quotes, tags, or links per work.
